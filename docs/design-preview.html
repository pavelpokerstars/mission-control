A toolbar of three, and everything else is contextual

  • The toolbar carries only what you might want from anywhere — Alerts, Later, Ask. The counts do real work: Alerts 3 · Later 2 tells you the state of things without opening anything. Ask 6 is the one that is not work — it says how much is in there, so it is never red.
  • An alert, a conversation and a record are never nav items. Each one is always about something specific, so you reach it by clicking that thing. Three is the ceiling here; a fifth entry and the launcher-for-tools look is back.
  • Sources is not in the toolbar — the connector dots on the right of the top bar are already the way there, and they double as its live status. A page you set up once does not need a permanent seat next to the pages you work in.
  • So only two kinds of back link survive, and both are contextual: ← back to the list from an alert, and ← all conversations from a chat. Later and Sources have none, because the toolbar already holds the way out.

Eight destinations became four

  • Nothing built is thrown away — it is re-pointed. /api/work becomes the list, /api/issue/:key feeds the alert's evidence, the summariser writes the claim, the graph becomes the Sources page and the "show me the loop" action.
  • The vault survives as a mechanism everywhere and one small page. Commitments are vault notes — the checklist on the missing-ticket alert is the vault. What went away is browsing it.
  • The test for a new page: can you name the moment somebody opens it, and what they do next? The five vendor panes fail that. All four survivors pass it.

One page per alert type — nothing inherited between them

Mission Control

The rules every alert page follows

  • The claim is one sentence, and it is the biggest thing on the page.
  • Every line of evidence names its source, its author and its date, and is clickable back to the record. Nothing is asserted without a receipt.
  • Four actions, and two of them are "no" — because they are different answers. Not now parks it: you write the note you will thank yourself for, choose when it comes back, and it lands in Later carrying this alert's own chip. Not needed dismisses it for good. Every note in Later that is tied to an issue got there this way; there is no other route.
  • The chat is at the bottom, not the top. You should not have to ask a question to find out what happened.
  • Asking is not a transition — try it. Click one of the suggested questions at the foot of the page. The answer appears where you are standing: no new page, no modal, no route change. The alert simply keeps going downwards.
  • The inline thread is the tail, not the archive. It shows the last couple of exchanges. Once a conversation is long enough that it would push the evidence off screen, you stop reading it here — which is what open full conversation in the header is for. It carries the count, so it stays honest as the thread grows.
  • That link is the only route to the full view, and it is why the full view can have a "back to the alert" button without lying. You went somewhere on purpose, so there is somewhere to come back from. Asking never moves you; opening the conversation does.

A page in its own right — every conversation, tied to an alert or not

Mission Control

Four rules the chat follows

  • It already knows what you are looking at. Opened from an alert it is scoped to that alert — you never restate the context. Opened from the list it answers across everything.
  • It cites, exactly like the page does. And when it is inferring rather than reading — "marcus probably owns this now" — it says so in the sentence.
  • When the answer is a shape, it draws the shape. Your own point from the 21st: chat is one-dimensional, and a dependency chain read left-to-right as prose is worse than seeing it.
  • Every answer can end in an action. The same three the page offers, reachable conversationally, so the chat is never a dead end you have to scroll back out of.

Tied or untied — the same chat either way

  • A list, then a chat — the same shape as Later. Ask opens on the conversations; clicking one opens it. No sidebar: two of four pages already work this way, and a third page shape would be a third thing to learn for no gain.
  • A conversation may be about an alert, or about nothing in particular. Each row says which, on its second line, and + New chat starts an untied one. Both read the same five sources, hold the same tools, cite the same way.
  • Being tied changes one thing: what it opened knowing. A tied chat starts with the alert's subject already loaded, so you never restate it. An untied chat starts cold and you say what you want. Nothing else differs.
  • "Back to the alert" is navigation, not behaviour. It appears only when there is an alert to go back to, and it changes nothing about how the chat answers — it is a shortcut to the page this conversation happens to be about.
  • Asking still never moves you. Questions asked on an alert are answered on the alert. This page is where a conversation lives once you open it deliberately, and where every past one is.

Retrieval, and one limit worth knowing

  • The subject is on the row, not in a heading. Each conversation says what it was about — "the Kafka thing" — because that is what you remember, not the date. The date is there, second.
  • A transcript is a poor record. This product exists because decisions get lost inside conversations, so it would be a strange thing to build and then bury its own conclusions in a chat log. When an exchange reaches something worth keeping it should offer to save the finding to Later: durable, cited, attached to the ticket rather than to a scroll position.
  • Already built: conversations persist in the browser (localStorage, last 30, auto-titled from your first message). They survive a reload, but they are per-browser — different laptop, no history. Fine for the hackathon; a real gap the day two people share an account.

You cannot read records here — and that is the point

  • It answers "what does it know?", never "what did they say?" Coverage and scope, not content. Click a row and you get what is in scope — which channels, which project, which board — not a list of messages. The moment you can click through to a message, you have rebuilt the Slack pane with an extra step in front of it.
  • The one exception is the failures, and it is an exception because it is repair, not browsing. You only ever see the records that did not join — never the 98% that did.
  • That block is nearly free to build. The connectors already drop these: an arrow whose ends do not both resolve to a ticket, a card with no key, a sticky that is empty after stripping. Today they are dropped silently. Counting them instead of discarding them is the whole change.
  • And it is the most honest thing in the product. "Three arrows point at something we cannot resolve" is a dependency somebody believes they expressed and the system will never see. A cross-origin iframe can never tell you that.
  • Nobody visits this twice. You set it up, and after that it is a place you point at rather than work in — which is exactly why it is safe to make it the impressive one, and where the animated brain belongs.
